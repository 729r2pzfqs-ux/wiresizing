About EMT (Electrical Metallic Tubing)

Thin-wall steel tubing, the most common raceway in commercial interiors and exposed residential work. Joined with set-screw or compression couplings, never threaded. Trade size 3-1/2" is covered by NEC Article 358, and its internal dimensions come from NEC Chapter 9, Table 4.

How the 40% conduit fill rule works

Conduit fill is a pure area calculation. Take the internal area of the raceway from NEC Chapter 9, Table 4, multiply by the allowable percentage from Table 1, then divide by the area of one conductor from Table 5. Round down, except that NEC Chapter 9, Note 7 lets you round up to the next whole number when the result has a decimal of 0.8 or more and all conductors are the same size.

Max conductors = conduit area × fill % ÷ conductor area (Note 7 rounding)

For 3-1/2" EMT with 12 AWG THHN: 11.545 × 0.40 ÷ 0.0133 = 347.22 → 347 conductors

The three percentages in NEC Chapter 9, Table 1 are not arbitrary. A single conductor gets 53% because it can be pulled straight down the middle. Exactly two conductors get only 31% because two circles inside a circle waste the most space. Three or more conductors nest efficiently again, so the limit rises to 40%.

Fill is only half the job. Once you have more than three current-carrying conductors in the same raceway, NEC 310.15(C)(1) also derates their ampacity — 80% for 4–6 conductors, 70% for 7–9, 50% for 10–20. A conduit can be legally filled and still leave your conductors undersized for the load. See NEC Table 310.15 for the full derating factors.

Nipples are the exception: a conduit or tubing length not exceeding 24 inches between boxes or enclosures may be filled to 60% per NEC Chapter 9, Table 1, Note 4 — and no ampacity derating applies to it. For 3-1/2" EMT that raises the usable area from 4.618 in² to 6.927 in².

Frequently Asked Questions

How many wires fit in 3-1/2-inch EMT conduit?

A 3-1/2-inch EMT conduit has 11.545 square inches of internal area. With more than two conductors the NEC limits fill to 40%, or 4.618 in². That works out to 347 #12 THHN, 476 #14 THHN, 219 #10 THHN, or 126 #8 THHN conductors.

How many 12 AWG THHN wires fit in 3-1/2-inch EMT?

347 conductors. Each 12 AWG THHN wire is 0.0133 in² per NEC Chapter 9, Table 5, and the 40% fill area of 3-1/2-inch EMT is 4.618 in².

What is the maximum fill percentage for conduit?

NEC Chapter 9, Table 1 sets three limits: 53% for a single conductor, 31% for exactly two conductors, and 40% for three or more. The lower two-conductor figure exists because two round conductors pack poorly inside a round raceway.

Does the equipment grounding conductor count toward conduit fill?

Yes. Every conductor in the raceway counts toward the cross-sectional fill calculation, including equipment grounding conductors and neutrals. Grounding conductors do not count as current-carrying conductors for the separate NEC 310.15(C)(1) derating calculation, but they still occupy space.
