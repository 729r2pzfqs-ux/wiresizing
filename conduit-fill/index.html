Conduit Fill Charts

How many wires fit in a conduit? Pick the conduit type and trade size for the full NEC fill chart. Internal areas come from NEC Chapter 9, Table 4, conductor areas from Table 5, and the fill percentages from Table 1 — 53% for one conductor, 31% for two, 40% for three or more.

Quick answer

A 1/2-inch EMT conduit holds 9 12 AWG THHN conductors or 12 14 AWG THHN conductors. A 3/4-inch EMT holds 16 12 AWG. A 1-inch EMT holds 26. Remember that a 12/2 cable pulled as loose conductors is three wires — hot, neutral, and ground — and all three count.

Fill is not the only limit

A raceway can pass the fill calculation and still be wrong. Three further rules apply to almost every real pull:

  • Ampacity derating. More than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway triggers NEC 310.15(C)(1): 80% for 4–6, 70% for 7–9, 50% for 10–20. See Table 310.15.
  • 360 degrees of bend. NEC 358.26 (and the equivalent section in each raceway article) limits a run between pull points to four quarter-bends, 360 degrees total.
  • Voltage drop. Fill says nothing about run length. Check the voltage drop formula or use the wire size calculator for long runs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum conduit fill allowed by the NEC?

NEC Chapter 9, Table 1 allows 53% fill for a single conductor, 31% for exactly two conductors, and 40% for three or more conductors. A conduit nipple 24 inches or shorter between enclosures may be filled to 60%.

How do you calculate conduit fill?

Multiply the conduit internal area from NEC Chapter 9, Table 4 by the allowable fill percentage, then divide by the area of one conductor from Table 5. Round down, or up when the decimal is 0.8 or more (Chapter 9, Note 7). For example, 1/2-inch EMT has 0.304 in² internal area; 40% of that is 0.122 in²; divided by the 0.0133 in² area of 12 AWG THHN gives 9 conductors.

How many wires can I put in 1/2-inch conduit?

In 1/2-inch EMT: 12 conductors of 14 AWG THHN, 9 of 12 AWG, 5 of 10 AWG, or 3 of 8 AWG. PVC Schedule 40 and rigid metal conduit hold slightly different counts because their internal diameters differ.

Does the ground wire count in conduit fill?

Yes. Equipment grounding conductors, neutrals, and all circuit conductors count toward the cross-sectional area fill calculation. Grounding conductors are excluded only from the separate count of current-carrying conductors used for ampacity derating under NEC 310.15(C)(1).

What happens if conduit is overfilled?

Overfilled conduit makes conductors difficult or impossible to pull without damaging insulation, traps heat, and is a code violation that will fail inspection. It also compounds the ampacity derating problem, since more conductors in a raceway means more mutual heating.
