NEC Table Reference

The National Electrical Code tables that get looked up by number, reproduced in full with the code sections that modify them. Values follow the 2020 and 2023 NEC.

NEC Table 310.16

Conductor Ampacity

Allowable ampacity of insulated conductors, copper and aluminum, at 60°C, 75°C and 90°C. The starting point for every branch circuit and feeder.

NEC Table 310.15

Correction & Adjustment Factors

Ambient temperature correction and the derating factors for more than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway — 80%, 70%, 50% and below.

NEC Table 250.66

Grounding Electrode Conductor

Sizes the conductor from the service enclosure to the ground rods, water pipe, or Ufer, based on the largest service-entrance conductor.

NEC Table 250.122

Equipment Grounding Conductor

Sizes the fault-current path from the rating of the overcurrent device — 14 AWG for 15 A, 12 AWG for 20 A, 10 AWG up to 60 A.

NEC Table 300.5

Minimum Burial Depths

Minimum cover for underground wiring: 24 inches direct burial, 18 inches PVC, 6 inches rigid metal, 12 inches for GFCI-protected residential circuits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which NEC table gives wire ampacity?

NEC Table 310.16. It lists allowable ampacity for insulated conductors rated up to 2000 volts with not more than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway or cable, at 30°C ambient, for copper and aluminum at 60°C, 75°C and 90°C.

Which NEC table gives ground wire size?

Two different tables. Table 250.122 sizes the equipment grounding conductor from the overcurrent device rating. Table 250.66 sizes the grounding electrode conductor from the service-entrance conductors. They answer different questions and are frequently confused.

Which NEC edition do these tables come from?

The values shown follow the 2020 and 2023 National Electrical Code, which share the same numbering for these tables. The 2017 edition numbered the derating tables differently — 310.15(B)(2)(a) and 310.15(B)(3)(a) instead of 310.15(B)(1) and 310.15(C)(1) — with the same values. Always confirm which edition your local jurisdiction has adopted.
