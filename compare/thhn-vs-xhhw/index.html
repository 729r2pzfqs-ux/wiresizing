THHN vs XHHW Wire

THHN and XHHW are the two most common building wire types used in commercial and residential electrical installations. Both are rated for 600V and 90°C in dry locations, but they use fundamentally different insulation systems — PVC with a nylon jacket (THHN) versus cross-linked polyethylene (XHHW) — which gives each distinct advantages depending on the environment.

THHN

Full name: Thermoplastic High Heat-resistant Nylon-coated

Insulation: PVC (thermoplastic) with nylon jacket

Temp rating: 90°C dry, 75°C wet (THWN-2: 90°C wet)

Voltage: 600V

Advantages

  • Lower cost per foot
  • Easier to strip and terminate
  • Thinner insulation profile (better conduit fill)
  • Nylon jacket resists abrasion and chemicals
  • Most common building wire in North America

XHHW

Full name: Cross-linked polyethylene High Heat-resistant Water-resistant

Insulation: XLPE (thermoset / cross-linked polyethylene)

Temp rating: 90°C dry, 75°C wet (XHHW-2: 90°C wet and dry)

Voltage: 600V (some rated 1000V)

Advantages

  • Superior moisture resistance
  • Higher dielectric strength
  • More durable insulation (thermoset won't melt)
  • Better for wet and damp locations
  • Suitable for direct burial with proper cable assembly

Both THHN and XHHW share the 90°C column but are typically derated to 75°C ampacity values per NEC 110.14(C), based on the temperature rating of terminations (lugs, breakers, outlets). The 90°C rating is still useful for ampacity correction and adjustment factor calculations.

When to Use Which?

Choose THHN When:

  • Standard commercial/residential conduit runs
  • Budget-sensitive projects
  • Dry indoor locations
  • Smaller conduit fill matters (thinner profile)
  • Easy stripping and termination is preferred

Choose XHHW When:

  • Wet locations (basements, outdoor conduit)
  • Industrial and harsh environments
  • Healthcare facilities (NEC 517.160, low dielectric constant)
  • Higher voltage applications (1000V ratings available)
  • Moisture resistance is critical

NEC Requirements: Both THHN and XHHW are listed in NEC Table 310.4(1). For ampacity purposes, NEC 110.14(C) typically limits both to 75°C column values unless all terminations are rated for 90°C. The 90°C rating is useful for ampacity correction and adjustment factor calculations per NEC 310.15(B). The THWN-2 and XHHW-2 variants are both rated 90°C in wet locations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can THHN wire be used outdoors?

THHN alone is rated for dry locations only at 90°C. However, most modern THHN is dual-rated as THHN/THWN-2, which is rated for wet locations at 90°C. Check the wire markings — if it says THWN-2, it can be used in wet and outdoor locations in conduit.

Is XHHW better than THHN?

XHHW has better moisture resistance and more durable insulation since XLPE won't melt like PVC can. For wet locations or demanding environments, XHHW is the better choice. For standard dry conduit runs, THHN works well and costs less.

What does the -2 mean in THWN-2 and XHHW-2?

The -2 suffix means the wire maintains its full 90°C temperature rating in wet locations. Without the -2, both THHN and XHHW are derated to 75°C in wet conditions.

Can I mix THHN and XHHW in the same conduit?

Yes. Both wire types can be installed in the same raceway. Use the ampacity column from NEC Table 310.16 that matches the lowest-rated conductor in the raceway, and apply conduit fill calculations per NEC 310.15(C).
