12 AWG vs 14 AWG Wire

The two most common residential wire sizes compared. 12 AWG is rated for 20-amp circuits and required for kitchen countertop and bathroom outlets. 14 AWG is rated for 15-amp circuits and typically used for lighting and general-purpose bedroom or living room outlets. The key difference comes down to ampacity, NEC overcurrent protection limits, and voltage drop performance.

12 AWG

Diameter: 2.053 mm (0.0808 in)

Cross-section: 3.31 mm²

Max breaker: 20A (NEC 240.4(D))

Resistance: 1.588 Ω/1000 ft

Common in: 20A outlets, kitchen circuits, garage, appliances

14 AWG

Diameter: 1.628 mm (0.0641 in)

Cross-section: 2.08 mm²

Max breaker: 15A (NEC 240.4(D))

Resistance: 2.525 Ω/1000 ft

Common in: 15A lighting circuits, bedrooms, general-purpose

When to Use Which?

Use 12 AWG When:

  • 20A kitchen countertop outlet circuits
  • Bathroom receptacle circuits
  • Garage and workshop circuits
  • Outdoor outlet circuits
  • Any circuit longer than 50 ft
  • Future-proofing for circuit upgrades

Use 14 AWG When:

  • 15A lighting circuits
  • Bedroom outlet circuits
  • Living room general-purpose circuits
  • Short runs under 50 ft with light loads
  • Cost savings are a priority on permitted circuits

NEC Requirements: NEC 240.4(D) mandates maximum overcurrent protection of 15A for 14 AWG and 20A for 12 AWG copper conductors. NEC 210.11 requires 20A circuits for kitchen countertop and bathroom receptacles. You cannot use 14 AWG wire on a 20A breaker — doing so is a code violation and a fire hazard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use 14 AWG wire on a 20 amp breaker?

No. NEC 240.4(D) limits 14 AWG to 15A overcurrent protection. Using 14 AWG on a 20A breaker is a code violation and fire hazard. The wire could overheat before the breaker trips, potentially causing a fire inside the wall.

Is 12 AWG wire overkill for lighting?

Not necessarily. While 14 AWG is code-compliant for 15A lighting circuits, 12 AWG provides a safety margin, reduces voltage drop on longer runs, and allows future circuit upgrades to 20A without rewiring. The added cost is typically modest compared to total project cost.

What is the voltage drop difference between 12 and 14 AWG?

At 100 feet on a 15A load, 14 AWG drops about 3.8% while 12 AWG drops about 2.4%. For runs over 50 feet, 12 AWG helps stay within the NEC-recommended 3% maximum voltage drop for branch circuits.

Which wire size does NEC require for kitchen outlets?

NEC 210.11(C)(1) requires at least two 20A small-appliance branch circuits for kitchen countertop receptacles, which means 12 AWG minimum. 14 AWG cannot be used for these circuits regardless of the load.
