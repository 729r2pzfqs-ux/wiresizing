Why 4/0 aluminum works here but not everywhere

This is the most misquoted number in residential wire sizing. In NEC Table 310.16, 4/0 AWG aluminum is rated 180 A at 75°C — not enough for a 200-amp circuit. 4/0 aluminum is nevertheless the standard 200-amp residential service conductor, and both facts are correct, because two different rules apply:

Application Governing rule Aluminum for 200 A
Dwelling service entrance, or the feeder carrying the entire dwelling load NEC 310.12 (the “83% rule”) 4/0 AWG
Everything else — sub-panel feeders, commercial feeders, branch circuits NEC Table 310.16 250 kcmil

NEC 310.12 permits service and main-power feeder conductors for a one-family dwelling to be sized at 83% of the service rating: 200 A × 0.83 = 166 A, and 4/0 aluminum’s 180 A rating covers that. The allowance exists because a dwelling never draws its full service rating continuously. It applies only to the conductors carrying the entire dwelling load.

For a 200-amp sub-panel feeder to a garage or shop, a commercial feeder, or any circuit that is not the dwelling’s main power feeder, 310.12 does not apply and you need 250 kcmil aluminum (205 A at 75°C). Copper is 4/0 AWG (230 A) in either case.

Installing aluminum correctly

  • Modern aluminum building wire must be AA-8000 series alloy (NEC 310.14(A)(1)). The 1960s-era AA-1350 alloy behind aluminum’s bad reputation is no longer permitted for branch circuits.
  • Terminate only on lugs and breakers marked AL/CU or AL9CU. Copper-only terminals are the most common failure point.
  • Apply an antioxidant compound to the stripped conductor before landing it, unless the termination is listed as not requiring it.
  • Torque every connection to the manufacturer’s value with a calibrated torque tool — NEC 110.14(D) makes this mandatory. Loose aluminum terminations are the primary cause of feeder failures.
  • Aluminum runs roughly half the weight and a third to half the cost of copper at feeder sizes, which is why it dominates service entrance and sub-panel feeders.

Frequently Asked Questions

What wire size do I need for 200 amps?

Under NEC Table 310.16 at 75°C, a 200-amp circuit needs 3/0 AWG copper (200A) or 250 kcmil aluminum (205A). For a one-family dwelling service or main power feeder, NEC 310.12 allows sizing at 83% (166A): 2/0 AWG copper or 4/0 AWG aluminum, which is why 4/0 aluminum is the standard 200A residential service conductor.

What breaker do I need for 200 amps?

A 200-amp circuit uses a 200A double-pole breaker. It protects 3/0 AWG copper or 250 kcmil aluminum under Table 310.16, or 2/0 copper or 4/0 aluminum on a dwelling service sized under NEC 310.12.

What size wire for a 200-amp service entrance?

A 200-amp dwelling service entrance uses 2/0 AWG copper or 4/0 AWG aluminum under NEC 310.12. Aluminum is standard for 200A service entrance conductors because of significant cost savings. 4/0 aluminum is rated 180 A in Table 310.16, below the 200 A service rating — it qualifies here only because NEC 310.12 lets a dwelling service conductor be sized at 83% of the service rating (200 A × 0.83 = 166 A). A 200-amp sub-panel feeder does not get that allowance and needs 250 kcmil aluminum. The conductors connect the utility meter to the main breaker panel.

Can a 200-amp panel handle an EV charger, electric range, and electric dryer?

Yes — a 200-amp panel easily handles these loads simultaneously. A 48A EV charger needs a 60A breaker (30% of the panel rating), a range uses a 50A breaker (25%), and a dryer uses a 30A breaker (15%). Total demand rarely exceeds 120–140A at any moment due to load diversity, leaving ample headroom.
