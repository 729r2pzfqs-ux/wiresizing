50-Amp Wire Size by Termination Rating

RuleCopperAluminum
60°C column — NM-B cable, or any termination not marked 75°C6 AWG4 AWG
75°C column — THHN/THWN-2 with 75°C-rated breaker and lugs8 AWG6 AWG

Most breakers and panelboards rated 100A or less are listed 60/75°C, but NM-B cable is always limited to the 60°C column (NEC 334.80). When in doubt, use the 60°C answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What wire size do I need for 50 amps?

Using the 60°C column of NEC Table 310.16, which NEC 110.14(C) requires by default for circuits of 100A or less and always for NM-B cable, a 50-amp circuit needs 6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum. With THHN/THWN-2 conductors and breakers and lugs rated 75°C, 8 AWG copper or 6 AWG aluminum is permitted.

What breaker do I need for 50 amps?

A 50-amp circuit uses a 50A double-pole circuit breaker. The breaker protects the 6 AWG copper (or 4 AWG aluminum) wire from overload. Double-pole breakers are used for 240V circuits and occupy two slots in the panel.

What wire size for a hot tub on a 50-amp circuit?

A 50-amp hot tub circuit requires 6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum wire on a 50A GFCI double-pole breaker. NEC 680.44 requires GFCI protection for hot tub feeders. The disconnect must be within sight of the spa, 5–50 feet away.

What wire size for a Level 2 EV charger?

EV chargers are continuous loads, so the circuit must be rated at 125% of the charger current (NEC 625.41). A 40A charger needs a 50A circuit: 6 AWG copper in NM-B, or 8 AWG THHN with 75°C terminations. A 48A charger needs a 60A circuit: 6 AWG THHN with 75°C terminations, or 4 AWG copper if the 60°C column applies (including NM-B).
