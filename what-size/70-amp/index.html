Most breakers and panelboards rated 100A or less are listed 60/75°C, but NM-B cable is always limited to the 60°C column (NEC 334.80). When in doubt, use the 60°C answer.

Aluminum Feeder Specifications

Aluminum has about 61% of copper’s conductivity, so a 70A feeder that takes 4 AWG copper needs 3 AWG aluminum — typically one to two gauge sizes larger.

Installing aluminum correctly

  • Modern aluminum building wire must be AA-8000 series alloy (NEC 310.14(A)(1)). The 1960s-era AA-1350 alloy behind aluminum’s bad reputation is no longer permitted for branch circuits.
  • Terminate only on lugs and breakers marked AL/CU or AL9CU. Copper-only terminals are the most common failure point.
  • Apply an antioxidant compound to the stripped conductor before landing it, unless the termination is listed as not requiring it.
  • Torque every connection to the manufacturer’s value with a calibrated torque tool — NEC 110.14(D) makes this mandatory. Loose aluminum terminations are the primary cause of feeder failures.
  • Aluminum runs roughly half the weight and a third to half the cost of copper at feeder sizes, which is why it dominates service entrance and sub-panel feeders.

Frequently Asked Questions

What wire size do I need for 70 amps?

Using the 60°C column of NEC Table 310.16, which NEC 110.14(C) requires by default for circuits of 100A or less and always for NM-B cable, a 70-amp circuit needs 4 AWG copper or 2 AWG aluminum. With THHN/THWN-2 conductors and breakers and lugs rated 75°C, 4 AWG copper or 3 AWG aluminum is permitted.

What breaker do I need for 70 amps?

A 70-amp circuit uses a 70A double-pole circuit breaker. The breaker protects the 4 AWG copper (or 3 AWG aluminum) wire from overload. Double-pole breakers are used for 240V circuits and occupy two slots in the panel.

What can a 70-amp sub-panel power?

A 70-amp sub-panel can power a large workshop, an addition to a home, or multiple high-draw appliances simultaneously. It supports 240V circuits for welders, air compressors, EV chargers, and heating units alongside 120V outlets and lighting.

Can I use 6 AWG wire for a 70-amp circuit?

No. 6 AWG copper is rated 65A at 75°C and 55A at 60°C, both below 70A. Use 4 AWG copper (70A at 60°C, 85A at 75°C), or 3 AWG aluminum with 75°C terminations (2 AWG aluminum at 60°C).
