Wire Types

The insulation type printed on a conductor decides three things: how hot it may run, whether it may get wet, and which ampacity column applies to it. Here is every common type side by side, with a full page for each.

The ampacity column is the one that catches people out. NM-B and UF-B contain 90°C conductors but are restricted to the 60°C column by NEC 334.80 and 340.80 respectively, which is why 6 AWG Romex will not carry a 60 A load while 6 AWG THHN in conduit will.

Reading the letter code

Wire type designations are built from a fixed set of letters. Once you know them, an unfamiliar marking parses on sight:

  • T — thermoplastic insulation (PVC)
  • H — heat resistant, 75°C; HH — high heat resistant, 90°C
  • W — suitable for wet locations
  • N — nylon outer jacket, abrasion and oil resistant
  • X — cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE), a thermoset insulation
  • U — underground; UF — underground feeder; USE — underground service entrance
  • R — rubber insulation (RHH, RHW)
  • -2 — rated 90°C in wet locations as well as dry

So THWN-2 reads as thermoplastic, heat resistant, wet rated, nylon jacketed, 90°C wet. XHHW-2 is cross-linked, high heat resistant, wet rated, 90°C wet. USE-2 is underground service entrance, 90°C wet.
