THHN and THWN-2 Wire — Specifications, Ampacity and Uses

THHN is Thermoplastic High Heat-resistant Nylon-coated: a single insulated conductor rated 600 V and 90°C in dry locations. Nearly everything sold today is dual-marked THHN/THWN-2, which adds a 90°C wet rating. It is the default wire for anything pulled through conduit, and it is a conductor rather than a cable — it always needs a raceway.

What the letters mean

T — thermoplastic insulation. H — heat resistant, good for 75°C. HH — high heat resistant, 90°C. W — suitable for wet locations. N — nylon outer jacket, which adds abrasion, oil and gas resistance and makes the wire slippery enough to pull easily.

The -2 suffix on THWN-2 means 90°C in both wet and dry locations. Plain THWN is only 75°C wet. Since almost all stock is dual-rated THHN/THWN-2, you get the 90°C wet rating whether you asked for it or not — but check the printing on the jacket rather than assuming.

Where THHN may and may not be used

THHN is a single conductor, not a cable assembly. It must be installed in a raceway — EMT, PVC, rigid metal, IMC, flexible metal conduit, or a wireway. It may not be run exposed, stapled to framing, or fished through walls the way NM-B cable can.

In wet locations, including any underground raceway, you need the THWN-2 rating. NEC 300.5(B) defines the interior of an underground raceway as a wet location, so plain THHN is not legal in buried PVC even though the pipe looks dry when you install it. Direct burial without a raceway is never permitted — that is what USE-2 and UF-B are for.

The 90°C rating is for derating, not for bigger breakers

The most common THHN mistake is loading a conductor to its 90°C ampacity. NEC 110.14(C) limits the circuit to the lowest temperature rating of any termination, and virtually every breaker and lug is listed at 75°C. So 12 AWG THHN is a 30 A conductor on paper, a 25 A conductor at the terminals, and a 20 A circuit once NEC 240.4(D) is applied.

What the 90°C rating actually buys you is headroom for derating. Nine conductors in a conduit derate to 70%: from the 90°C column that is 30 × 0.70 = 21 A, which still supports a 20 A circuit. From the 75°C column it would be 17.5 A and you would be forced up to 10 AWG.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is THHN wire used for?

THHN/THWN-2 is the standard building wire pulled through conduit for branch circuits, feeders, and service conductors in commercial and residential construction, and for machine tool and panel wiring. It is a single conductor and always requires a raceway.

Is THHN rated for wet locations?

Plain THHN is dry-location only. THWN is rated 75°C wet and THWN-2 is rated 90°C wet. Almost all wire sold today is dual-marked THHN/THWN-2 and is therefore fine in wet locations, but you should confirm the marking printed on the jacket.

Can THHN be buried directly in the ground?

No. THHN and THWN-2 are single conductors that must be installed in a raceway. For direct burial use UF-B cable for branch circuits or USE-2 for service and feeder conductors.

What is the difference between THHN and THWN?

The W means suitable for wet locations. THHN is 90°C dry only; THWN is 75°C wet; THWN-2 is 90°C in both wet and dry. The physical wire is normally the same product carrying all three markings.

What is the ampacity of 12 AWG THHN?

30 A at the 90°C column of NEC Table 310.16. In practice the usable figure is 25 A because terminations are rated 75°C, and the breaker is limited to 20 A by NEC 240.4(D). The 90°C value is used as the starting point for derating calculations.
