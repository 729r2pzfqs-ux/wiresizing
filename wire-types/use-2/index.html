USE-2 Wire — Underground Service Entrance Cable

USE-2 is Underground Service Entrance cable, rated 600 V and 90°C in wet locations, and listed for direct burial without a raceway. That last point is what makes it different from THWN-2 and XHHW-2. Its limitation is the mirror image: USE-2 has no flame-retardant rating, so it may not be run inside a building unless it is also marked RHH or RHW-2.

The flame rating limitation

This is the rule that catches people. Straight USE-2 with no additional marking is an outdoor and underground conductor. NEC 338.12(B)(1) prohibits underground service-entrance cable from being used for interior wiring, because the insulation has no flame-retardant additive and will propagate fire along a wall cavity.

Most USE-2 sold in the US is dual- or triple-marked USE-2 / RHH / RHW-2. The RHH and RHW-2 markings are flame-rated building wire types, and that marking is what makes it legal to continue the run inside the building. Read the print on the insulation before you buy — the two products look identical on the reel.

USE-2 versus UF-B versus direct-burial conduit

USE-2 is a single conductor for service and feeder work. You bury three or four of them in a trench. It is 90°C wet-rated, so it derates well, and it is what the utility side of a service lateral is usually made of.

UF-B is a multi-conductor cable assembly for branch circuits. It is limited to the 60°C column, so it needs a larger conductor for the same load, but it is a complete cable with a ground included and it is what you use for a circuit out to a shed or a post light.

THWN-2 or XHHW-2 in PVC conduit is the third option and often the best one for anything you might want to change later. It costs more up front, buries shallower under NEC Table 300.5 — 18 inches for PVC against 24 for direct burial — and lets you pull new conductors without digging.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can USE-2 be direct buried?

Yes. USE-2 is listed for direct burial without a raceway, which is its defining feature. Minimum cover is 24 inches in the general case per NEC Table 300.5(A).

Can USE-2 be run inside a building?

Only if it is dual-marked RHH or RHW-2. Straight USE-2 has no flame-retardant rating and NEC 338.12(B)(1) prohibits it for interior wiring. Most USE-2 on the market is marked USE-2/RHH/RHW-2, which is permitted inside.

What is the difference between USE-2 and THWN-2?

USE-2 is listed for direct burial with no raceway; THWN-2 is not and always requires a conduit. THWN-2 is flame rated and may be run inside buildings; straight USE-2 may not. Both are 90°C wet rated.

Is USE-2 the same as UF-B?

No. USE-2 is a single conductor for service and feeder use. UF-B is a multi-conductor branch circuit cable limited to the 60°C ampacity column. Both are direct-burial rated.
