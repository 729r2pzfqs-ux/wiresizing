SOOW and SJOOW Portable Cord — Ampacity and Ratings

SOOW and SJOOW are flexible portable cords covered by NEC Article 400. The letters are a specification, not a brand: S for 600 V service cord, J for the 300 V junior version, OO for oil-resistant jacket and oil-resistant insulation, and W for weather and water resistance including sunlight. Ampacity comes from NEC Table 400.5(A)(1), not from Table 310.16.

A grounding conductor is not counted as current-carrying. These values are materially lower than the building-wire figures in Table 310.16 — do not substitute one table for the other.

Reading the letter codes

S — hard service cord, 600 V. SJ — junior hard service, 300 V, thinner jacket, lighter duty. E — thermoplastic elastomer insulation. O — oil-resistant jacket. OO — oil-resistant jacket and oil-resistant conductor insulation. W — weather and water resistant, and suitable for outdoor use including sunlight exposure.

So SOOW is 600 V, oil resistant inside and out, weather rated. SJOOW is the same construction at 300 V. SOW would be oil-resistant jacket only. The order of the letters is fixed by the standard, which is why the codes look cryptic but parse cleanly once you know the key.

Cord is not fixed wiring

NEC 400.12 is explicit about what flexible cord may not do, and it is the most violated rule in Article 400. Cord may not be used as a substitute for the fixed wiring of a structure. It may not be run through holes in walls, ceilings or floors, or through doorways and windows. It may not be concealed behind walls or above suspended ceilings, or attached to building surfaces except where specifically permitted.

The permitted uses in NEC 400.10 are the mirror image: pendants, wiring of luminaires, connection of portable equipment, elevator cables, connection of appliances designed for ready removal, and equipment that must be moved for maintenance. If the load is not intended to move or be removed, it needs building wire in a raceway or a cable assembly, not cord.

Ampacity comes from a different table

Because cord conductors are fine-stranded and bundled in a flexible jacket with no air gap, they use NEC Table 400.5(A)(1) rather than Table 310.16. Column A applies to cords with two current-carrying conductors, Column B to cords with three. Cords with four to six current-carrying conductors derate to 80%, and seven to nine to 70%.

The values are meaningfully lower than building wire. 12 AWG SOOW with three conductors is 20 A, matching 12 AWG THHN by coincidence — but 6 AWG SOOW is 45 A where 6 AWG THHN in conduit is 65 A. Size portable cord from Table 400.5, never from the building wire tables.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does SOOW stand for?

S is 600 V hard service cord, the first O means an oil-resistant jacket, the second O means oil-resistant conductor insulation, and W means weather and water resistant including sunlight exposure.

What is the difference between SOOW and SJOOW?

Voltage rating and jacket thickness. SOOW is rated 600 V for hard service; SJOOW is the "junior" version rated 300 V with a thinner jacket for lighter duty. The oil and weather ratings are identical.

What is the ampacity of 12 AWG SOOW cord?

20 amps with three current-carrying conductors, or 25 amps with two, per NEC Table 400.5(A)(1). Cord ampacity is lower than building wire of the same gauge because the conductors are bundled inside a flexible jacket with no air gap.

Can SOOW cord be used for permanent wiring?

No. NEC 400.12 prohibits flexible cord as a substitute for the fixed wiring of a structure, and prohibits running it through walls, ceilings, floors, doorways or windows. It is for portable equipment, pendants, and connections to appliances designed for ready removal.
