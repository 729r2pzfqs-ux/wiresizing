The recommended conductor relies on the 75°C column and needs THHN/THWN-2 with breaker and lugs rated 75°C. With NM-B cable or 60°C terminations, use 4 AWG copper (NEC 110.14(C)(1)(a), 334.80).

Always size a circuit from the equipment nameplate or a load calculation, not from a general table. Air conditioners and heat pumps in particular carry a minimum circuit ampacity (MCA) and maximum overcurrent protective device (MOCP) rating that override the general rules.

NEC requirements for this circuit

60 A is where the difference between wire types matters most. 6 AWG copper THHN on 75°C terminations gives 65 A and works. 6 AWG NM-B is limited to the 60°C column at 55 A and does not work — a 60 A circuit in NM-B cable needs 4 AWG.

Outdoor receptacle outlets up to 250 V and 50 A require GFCI under NEC 210.8. Sub-panel feeders themselves do not, but remember a sub-panel must have its neutral and ground bars separated, with four conductors in the feeder (NEC 250.32).

Single-phase, 240V, full 60 A load, 0.491 Ω/1000 ft DC resistance (NEC Chapter 9, Table 8) at 75°C terminations. NEC 210.19(A) Informational Note recommends keeping branch-circuit drop at or below 3%, and 5% total across feeder plus branch circuit. See the voltage drop formula for the arithmetic.

The 80% rule: a continuous load is one expected to run for three hours or more — EV chargers, electric heat, and most commercial lighting. NEC 210.20(A) requires the overcurrent device to be rated at not less than 125% of that continuous load, which leaves 48 A usable on a 60 A breaker. A breaker that trips after an hour or two under a steady load is usually a continuous-load sizing mistake, not a faulty breaker.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size wire do I need for a 60 amp breaker?

6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum, per NEC Table 310.16 at the 75°C column. The equipment grounding conductor is 10 AWG copper per NEC Table 250.122.

What is the maximum continuous load on a 60 amp breaker?

48 amps. NEC 210.19(A)(1) and 210.20(A) require the circuit to be rated at 125% of a continuous load — one that runs for three hours or more — which works out to 80% of the breaker rating available for continuous use. Non-continuous loads may use the full 60 A.

What size ground wire for a 60 amp breaker?

10 AWG copper or 8 AWG aluminum, from NEC Table 250.122. The table lists maximum overcurrent device ratings, so you round up to the next listed row. If the circuit conductors are upsized for voltage drop, NEC 250.122(B) requires the ground to be upsized proportionally too.

Can I use 4 AWG NM-B cable on a 60 amp breaker?

Yes. NEC 334.80 requires NM-B ampacity to be taken from the 60°C column of Table 310.16, where 4 AWG copper is rated 70 A. That is why NM-B sometimes needs a larger conductor than THHN in conduit for the same breaker.
