The recommended conductor meets the 60°C column of NEC Table 310.16, so it works with NM-B cable and with any breaker or lug. NEC 110.14(C)(1)(a) requires the 60°C column at 100 A or less unless every termination is rated 75°C.

Always size a circuit from the equipment nameplate or a load calculation, not from a general table. Air conditioners and heat pumps in particular carry a minimum circuit ampacity (MCA) and maximum overcurrent protective device (MOCP) rating that override the general rules.

NEC requirements for this circuit

15 A is the smallest standard branch circuit in a dwelling. NEC 240.4(D)(3) caps 14 AWG copper at 15 A even though Table 310.16 rates it 20 A at 75°C — this is why you can never legally put 14 AWG on a 20 A breaker.

GFCI required for receptacles in bathrooms, garages, outdoors, crawl spaces, unfinished basements, kitchens, laundry areas, and within 6 feet of any sink (NEC 210.8). AFCI required for most dwelling living areas (NEC 210.12).

Single-phase, 120V, full 15 A load, 3.14 Ω/1000 ft DC resistance (NEC Chapter 9, Table 8) at 75°C terminations. NEC 210.19(A) Informational Note recommends keeping branch-circuit drop at or below 3%, and 5% total across feeder plus branch circuit. See the voltage drop formula for the arithmetic.

The 80% rule: a continuous load is one expected to run for three hours or more — EV chargers, electric heat, and most commercial lighting. NEC 210.20(A) requires the overcurrent device to be rated at not less than 125% of that continuous load, which leaves 12 A usable on a 15 A breaker. A breaker that trips after an hour or two under a steady load is usually a continuous-load sizing mistake, not a faulty breaker.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size wire do I need for a 15 amp breaker?

14 AWG copper or 12 AWG aluminum, per NEC Table 310.16 at the 75°C column. The equipment grounding conductor is 14 AWG copper per NEC Table 250.122.

What is the maximum continuous load on a 15 amp breaker?

12 amps. NEC 210.19(A)(1) and 210.20(A) require the circuit to be rated at 125% of a continuous load — one that runs for three hours or more — which works out to 80% of the breaker rating available for continuous use. Non-continuous loads may use the full 15 A.

What size ground wire for a 15 amp breaker?

14 AWG copper or 12 AWG aluminum, from NEC Table 250.122. The table lists maximum overcurrent device ratings, so you round up to the next listed row. If the circuit conductors are upsized for voltage drop, NEC 250.122(B) requires the ground to be upsized proportionally too.

Can I use 14 AWG NM-B cable on a 15 amp breaker?

Yes. NEC 334.80 requires NM-B ampacity to be taken from the 60°C column of Table 310.16, where 14 AWG copper is rated 15 A. That is why NM-B sometimes needs a larger conductor than THHN in conduit for the same breaker.
